Motivation to Attend the Workshop: Geometry of Instantons and Quivers
My academic background is primarily in computer science, complemented by a strong interest in
mathematics. Over time, I have developed a growing curiosity about the deep connections between
algebra and geometry. The theme of the workshop, Geometry of Instantons and Quivers, lies at a
fascinating intersection of these areas and offers an opportunity to explore advanced mathematical
structures that I have only encountered at an introductory level.
From a computational perspective, quivers and their representations are particularly intriguing
because they provide a combinatorial framework for studying complex geometric and algebraic
objects. Concepts such as moduli spaces, representation theory, and geometric structures arising
from quivers resonate with my interest in structured systems, networks, and abstract models that
often appear in theoretical computer science. Learning how these ideas connect to instanton moduli
spaces and gauge-theoretic geometry would greatly broaden my understanding of modern
mathematical methods.
I am especially motivated to attend this workshop to gain exposure to the geometric and physical
motivations behind instantons, as well as the role of quiver varieties in organizing and studying
these structures. Engaging with experts in the field and participating in discussions would help me
build a clearer conceptual foundation and identify potential directions where computational
techniques and mathematical theory interact.
Furthermore, the workshop would provide a valuable opportunity to bridge my computer science
training with more advanced areas of mathematics. I hope to develop a deeper appreciation of the
geometric and representation-theoretic tools used in this area and to understand how these ideas
may connect to algorithmic, combinatorial, or computational viewpoints.
Overall, attending this workshop would allow me to expand my mathematical perspective, learn
from leading researchers, and explore an area that sits at the crossroads of geometry and algebra,
and theoretical Computer Science.
